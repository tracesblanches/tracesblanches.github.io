Nordic skiing, even without a signal

Plan the day, keep to the route, be back before dark, with gloves on and no network. Traces Blanches puts the map of your ski area on your phone before you leave, anywhere in France, and guides you by voice with the phone in your pocket.

All of France, resorts and beyond

Traces Blanches covers the Nordic ski areas of the whole of France. The census counts 219 of them, drawn from OpenStreetMap and OpenSkiMap: in the Jura, the Vosges, the Massif Central, the Northern and Southern Alps, the Pyrenees, and as far as the Ardennes and the Morvan.

The app also covers Nordic backcountry skiing outside groomed areas: 41 areas and the great traverses, such as the Grande Traversée du Jura or the high plateaus of the Vercors.

Each area has its pack: the map and everything needed to ski without a network. A server rebuilds the packs every day with the estimated snow and the weather, and the app downloads only what has changed. Between areas, a light map of France lets you scroll across the whole country.

A day on skis

Here is a day with the Android app, from the evening before to the return. Every feature, explained

1

The evening before, then the morning

The evening before, on charge and on Wi-Fi, the app prepares the next day. In the morning one screen proposes the day: a loop, its time, the latest time to be back, and the reason for each choice. You confirm it with one gesture.

The route on the trails, its climb, its estimated time and the turnaround time are computed on the phone, in any area: you turn back 30 minutes before the end of civil twilight.

2

On the trail, phone in your pocket

A voice gives the next direction and the time left, then stays quiet while the trail is obvious. It speaks up for what matters: off route, a slope over 30°, a protected area, the turnaround time.

If a trail is closed or the group falls behind, the app proposes a shorter loop out loud; one gesture refuses it. The voice speaks French or English and needs no network. In fog, a bearing screen points to a hut, the car park or any point of the map.

3

The way back

With children you go slower, stop, play. The estimated time learns from your outings and adapts to the group of the day, and the arrival time is recalculated as you go.

"Take me back" guides you back along the way you skied. Before the time to be back, a reminder offers you a ready-to-send "back home" text message for the person you chose in the morning. At home, you can share the outing as a GPX or FIT file, or write it to Health Connect.

Slopes and avalanches, honestly

The map marks the slopes steeper than 30°, measured from the laser survey of IGN, the French mapping agency. It also estimates avalanche exposure from terrain and forest, with a method published by avalanche researchers and a published runout model. On the trail, the voice warns you before a slope over 30°.

Where Météo-France publishes an avalanche bulletin for the massif, the map shows it with its date. It also marks the areas with no mobile network at all, 112 included, from the ARCEP coverage maps.

Traces Blanches never presents a slope as free of avalanche risk, as the avalanche terrain scale advises. It replaces neither the avalanche bulletin, nor your judgement on the spot, nor an avalanche transceiver, nor training.

Nature and its rules

Reserves and protected areas have sensitive months, and their rules are written in official orders. Each pack holds the protected areas of the national inventory of natural heritage, and the general rules on bivouac and fires that apply across France.

Local orders are added from their text. A touch on an area of the map opens its card, with its rules.

Your position, your tracks and your day plans stay on your phone. There is no public map of off-piste outings.

Questions

Can I install the app?

Not yet: it comes out in winter 2027. The button below lets you follow its release.

Does it work without a network?

Yes. You download the pack of your ski area before you leave; after that, the phone needs no network.

Which phones?

Android phones running Android 10 or later. There is no iPhone version. A watch is not needed.
