Sources

The calculations of the app rest on published work. Here it is, with what the app takes from each.

Scientific papers

  1. Statham, G. and Campbell, C. (2025). The Avalanche Terrain Exposure Scale (ATES) v.2. Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences, 25, 1113–1137. DOI 10.5194/nhess-25-1113-2025 What the app takes: the avalanche terrain exposure scale the map follows, and why it never presents terrain as out of reach of avalanches.
  2. Toft, H. B., Sykes, J., Schauer, A., Hendrikx, J. and Hetland, A. (2024). AutoATES v2.0: Automated Avalanche Terrain Exposure Scale mapping. Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences, 24, 1779–1793. DOI 10.5194/nhess-24-1779-2024 What the app takes: the method that finds avalanche release areas from terrain and forest, then rates the terrain.
  3. Sykes, J., Toft, H., Haegeli, P. and Statham, G. (2024). Automated Avalanche Terrain Exposure Scale (ATES) mapping – local validation and optimization in western Canada. Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences, 24, 947–971. DOI 10.5194/nhess-24-947-2024 What the app takes: how this method compares with maps drawn by hand by experts.
  4. Larsen, H. T., Hendrikx, J., Slåtten, M. S. and Engeset, R. V. (2020). Developing nationwide avalanche terrain maps for Norway. Natural Hazards, 103, 2829–2847. DOI 10.1007/s11069-020-04104-7 What the app takes: the first version of the method, applied to the whole of Norway.
  5. D'Amboise, C. J. L., Neuhauser, M., Teich, M., Huber, A., Kofler, A., Perzl, F., Fromm, R., Kleemayr, K. and Fischer, J.-T. (2022). Flow-Py v1.0: a customizable, open-source simulation tool to estimate runout and intensity of gravitational mass flows. Geoscientific Model Development, 15, 2423–2439. DOI 10.5194/gmd-15-2423-2022 What the app takes: the model that follows an avalanche downhill, as far as it can run.
  6. Sollie, O., Gløersen, Ø., Gilgien, M. and Losnegard, T. (2021). Differences in pacing pattern and sub-technique selection between young and adult competitive cross-country skiers. Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports, 31, 553–563. DOI 10.1111/sms.13887 What the app takes: the only published measurement of young skiers' pace against adults, uphill, on the flat and downhill.
  7. Bolger, C. M., Kocbach, J., Hegge, A. M. and Sandbakk, Ø. (2015). Speed and heart-rate profiles in skating and classical cross-country skiing competitions. International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance, 10, 873–880. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2014-0335 What the app takes: the speed gap between skating and classic, by slope.
  8. Moxnes, J. F., Sandbakk, Ø. and Hausken, K. (2013). A simulation of cross-country skiing on varying terrain by using a mathematical power balance model. Open Access Journal of Sports Medicine, 4, 127–139. DOI 10.2147/OAJSM.S39843 What the app takes: working out time from the skier's effort, the slope, the glide and the air.
  9. Colbeck, S. C. (1994). A review of the friction of snow skis. Journal of Sports Sciences, 12, 285–295. DOI 10.1080/02640419408732174 What the app takes: how glide changes with temperature and snow.
  10. Almqvist, A., Pellegrini, B., Lintzén, N., Emami, N., Holmberg, H.-C. and Larsson, R. (2022). A Scientific Perspective on Reducing Ski-Snow Friction to Improve Performance in Olympic Cross-Country Skiing, the Biathlon and Nordic Combined. Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, 4, 844883. DOI 10.3389/fspor.2022.844883 What the app takes: the same question, with recent measurements.

Methods, scales and official data

  • AutoATES v3.0 (ISSW 2026 version). AutoATES group, open code under the MIT licence. What the app takes: the version of the method the app follows, with its published settings, nothing retuned.
  • AvaFrame, com4FlowPy. AvaFrame consortium, open code under the EUPL-1.2 licence. What the app takes: the code of the Flow-Py runout model, which the app rewrote and compared cell by cell.
  • Grading system for Nordic backcountry skiing, 2025. Alpine clubs practising Nordic backcountry skiing, meeting in Chambéry on 11 and 12 October 2025; published by skirandonneenordique.com. What the app takes: the four levels SRN1 to SRN4 and their limits of slope, distance and duration, the basis of the difficulty of a route.
  • Ski touring grades. Camptocamp, help article. What the app takes: the 30° limit of the beginner grade, which marks the end of Nordic backcountry terrain.
  • Munter method for touring time. Werner Munter, Swiss guide; explained by Backcountry Access (the original publication is not online). What the app takes: the effort time of an off-piste route, counted from distance and climb.
  • Snowpack simulation model data. Météo-France, on data.gouv.fr, Licence Ouverte 2.0. What the app takes: the snow depth simulated each day by massif, altitude and aspect, which the map spreads over the terrain.
  • snowtools, massif outlines. CNRM (Météo-France and CNRS), CeCILL-C licence. What the app takes: the outlines of the simulation's massifs, the Jura included.
  • Avalanche risk bulletin (BERA). Météo-France, Licence Ouverte 2.0. What the app takes: the bulletin of the massif, shown with its date in the Alps, the Pyrenees and Corsica.
  • Ministerial answer on the Jura, Journal officiel of 11 June 2024. French National Assembly, written question no. 14701. What the app takes: why the Jura has no BERA: Météo-France issues warning messages there instead.

Maps on this site

The maps on this site come from packs of several massifs (Hautes-Alpes, Vosges, Ariège Pyrenees, Haut-Jura) and from the census of Nordic ski areas. Each caption carries a short credit; here are the full sources.

  • Base map: IGN, Plan IGN v2, Licence Ouverte 2.0.
  • Slopes and terrain: IGN, LiDAR HD terrain model, Licence Ouverte 2.0.
  • Trails: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0, daily OpenSnowMap extract; Nordic centres: Protomaps © OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Grande Traversée du Jura: route from DATAtourisme, Licence Ouverte 2.0, and © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0; nearby routes from Camptocamp contributors, CC BY-SA 3.0.
  • Huts: © refuges.info contributors, CC BY-SA.
  • No-signal areas: ARCEP, Mon Réseau Mobile, maps of 30 June 2026, Licence Ouverte.
  • Map of France: Nordic ski areas © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0, OpenSkiMap; Nordic backcountry areas from OpenStreetMap, Geotrek and DATAtourisme; packs published: catalogue of the pack server on 21 November 2026; department outlines: Etalab administrative outlines (IGN Admin Express and OpenStreetMap), ODbL 1.0.

No map on this site shows a source whose licence forbids sharing it.
