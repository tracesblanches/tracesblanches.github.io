Offline

What works without a signal?

Everything in the site's pack: the base maps, the trails, the slopes, avalanche exposure, protected areas, huts, no-signal areas, estimated snow and the voice clips. Routing will run on the phone, with no server. Your position comes from satellites, which need no network.

What do I download before leaving?

The pack of the site where you ski. Today it weighs about 40 MB for Cervières and 35 MB for Chapelle-des-Bois. In the app, you will download it before you leave. Updating only the day's layers (snow, bulletin, weather) is planned.

What if the pack is a few days old?

Each layer carries its date and how long it stays valid. Slopes and avalanche exposure depend on terrain and forest: they do not change from one day to the next. Protected areas need a refresh every month. Snow, the avalanche bulletin and the weather age in hours or days: the app will show their age next to the information, so that you can judge whether to still trust it.

Battery

How long does the battery last?

We do not know yet: the measurement has not been made. Test outings in deep cold are planned this winter, in Cervières in January and in Chapelle-des-Bois in February and March. The figures will be published here.

What does the app do to save it?

What is planned: voice guidance with the screen off, adaptive GPS, stops detected by the accelerometer, altitude read from the barometer. The app will forecast the battery left at the end of the outing and keep a reserve for an emergency call.

Avalanches

Does the app tell me whether a slope is safe?

No, never. No map can tell you that. traceblanche shows where the terrain is steep and where a model finds it exposed. It shows the day's avalanche bulletin when Météo-France publishes one. The decision stays yours, on the spot.

Where do the slopes come from?

From the IGN LiDAR HD terrain model, at 5 m resolution. The pack sorts them into four bands: 30 to 35°, 35 to 40°, 40 to 45°, 45° and more. A slope under 30° can still lie below a steeper one: look above you too.

What is the avalanche exposure in the pack?

A computation, not an observation. The core finds possible release areas from terrain and forest (AutoATES v3.0 method), then follows the path of an avalanche downhill (Flow-Py method). Both methods are ported to Rust. The comparison with recorded avalanches is in progress. An avalanche can start where the model does not see one.

And the avalanche bulletin?

The pack takes the Météo-France avalanche bulletin (BERA) for the site's massif, with its date. The Jura has no BERA: for Chapelle-des-Bois, the pack points to the Météo-France mountain weather and to the SLF bulletin, which covers the neighbouring Swiss Jura.

Does the app plan off-piste routes?

The core can already do it: it avoids slopes over 30°, refuses areas closed on the chosen date and takes the estimated snow into account. In the app, this feature is planned for a second version.

Data and licences

Where does the data come from?

From public and open sources: IGN, OpenStreetMap, Météo-France, ARCEP, the French national natural heritage inventory (INPN), refuges.info and Camptocamp, among others. The licences and sources page lists them all, with their licence and the credit to show.

Why is trail status missing?

Ski areas publish the state of their trails without a licence that allows reuse. We read it for personal use, but it goes into no published pack. We will ask the Espace Nordique Jurassien and Nordic France for written permission.

Is the resort's trail map in the app?

No. A trail map drawn by a resort belongs to it, and we never redistribute it. The app's trails come from OpenStreetMap. Work is planned to carry the information of a trail map (name, colour, direction) over to our own lines, without copying the picture.

A trail is wrong or missing. What can I do?

If it is wrong in OpenStreetMap, fix it there: the fix will reach the next pack. Otherwise, open an issue with the “Données ou site” (data or website) form, with the place and the date you saw the problem.

Price

How much will the app cost?

The intended price is a single payment, for example €4.99. There will be no subscription and no ads. The project avoids any service that would cost money per user and per year: that is what makes a single payment possible.

Why pay for free software?

The payment funds the work on the app. The code stays under GPL-3.0-or-later: you can read it, change it and build the app yourself.

Will uploading to Strava cost extra?

No. Strava's API terms forbid charging for what goes through it. Uploading an outing to Strava, planned and optional, will stay outside what you pay for.

Other questions

Which places are covered?

Today, two test sites: Cervières (Hautes-Alpes) and Chapelle-des-Bois (Doubs). The census counts 219 Nordic ski areas in France; their packs come next.

What about the iPhone?

Later. The Android app comes first. The code licence already allows distribution through the App Store.

Do I need a watch?

No. The phone alone is enough. A Wear OS watch app is planned as an option, for comfort: heading, time left, vibrations.

How do I ask another question?

Open an issue on GitHub. Answers that help everyone will come to this page.
