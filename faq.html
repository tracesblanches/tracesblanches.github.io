Offline

What works without a signal?

Everything in the pack of your ski area: the map, the trails, the slopes, avalanche exposure, protected areas, huts, no-signal areas, estimated snow and the voice. The route is worked out on the phone. Your position comes from satellites, which need no network.

What do I download before leaving?

The pack of the area where you ski. Most packs weigh less than 10 MB; the largest, about 50 MB. You download it in the app, at the chalet or at home, before you leave. A server rebuilds the packs every day; the app updates the installed packs by downloading only what has changed, when there is a network and the battery is not low.

What if the pack is a few days old?

Each piece of information carries its date. Slopes and avalanche exposure depend on terrain and forest: they do not change from one day to the next. Protected areas need an update every month. Snow, the avalanche bulletin and the weather age in hours or days. Each card gives the source and the date of its information, and the app shows until when the pack is valid.

Battery

How long does the battery last?

We do not know yet: it has not been measured.

What does the app do to save it?

Voice guidance with the screen off; positions read in batches while the trail is obvious, every second near a junction; during a stop, satellites off, and the motion sensor wakes the outing when you move on. No timer during the outing. Outside it, only the update of the installed packs runs, when there is a network and the battery is not low.

Avalanches

Does the app tell me whether a slope can avalanche?

No map can rule it out, and the app never claims to. Traces Blanches shows where the terrain is steep and where a computation finds it exposed. It shows the day's avalanche bulletin when Météo-France publishes one. The app replaces neither the bulletin nor your judgement on the spot: the decision stays yours.

Where do the slopes come from?

From the laser survey of IGN, the French mapping agency, with one measurement every 5 m. The map sorts them into four bands: 30 to 35°, 35 to 40°, 40 to 45°, 45° and more. A slope under 30° can still lie below a steeper one: look above you too.

What is the avalanche exposure on the map?

A computation, not an observation. It finds the places where an avalanche could start, from terrain and forest, then follows its path downhill. It uses two methods published by researchers: AutoATES for the release areas and Flow-Py for the runout. The canopy height of IGN gives the forest. An avalanche can start where the computation does not see one.

And the avalanche bulletin?

The pack takes the Météo-France avalanche bulletin (BERA) for the area's massif, with its date. The Jura has no BERA: Météo-France issues warning messages there, and the pack points to the Météo-France mountain weather and to the bulletin of the Swiss institute SLF, which covers the neighbouring Swiss Jura. To learn to read a bulletin, the ANENA runs courses.

Your data

Is my position sent anywhere?

Not without you. Your position, your tracks and your day plans stay on your phone. The plan of the day leaves it only if you send it yourself, as a text message. In a group, your position goes by Bluetooth to the phones of the group only. A report you make goes to the server with its position.

Do I need an account?

No. The app downloads packs without an account. It carries no ads and no audience measurement.

Who sees my off-piste outings?

Nobody but you. There is no public map of off-piste outings. The privacy page details what stays on the phone and what the server receives.

Price

How much does the app cost?

A modest price, with no subscription and no ads. It covers the hosting: the server that builds and updates the packs every day. The app comes out in winter 2027; the Get the app page lets you follow its release.

Why pay if all the work is public?

The price only covers the hosting. All the work stays public: anyone can look at it and check what the app does.

Phones

Which phones does the app run on?

Android phones, from Android 10.

What about the iPhone?

There is no iPhone version.

Do I need a watch?

No. The phone alone is enough.

Other questions

Which places are covered?

The whole of France. The census counts 219 Nordic ski areas, from the Ardennes to the Pyrenees, and 41 Nordic backcountry areas and the great traverses outside resorts, such as the Grande Traversée du Jura. Every area has its pack, updated every day. The features page shows the map.

Where do the maps and information come from?

From public and open sources: IGN, OpenStreetMap, Météo-France, ARCEP, the French national natural heritage inventory, refuges.info and Camptocamp, among others. The sources and credits page names them all, with the papers the calculations rest on.

Why is trail status missing?

Ski areas publish the state of their trails without allowing its reuse. So it is not in the app.

Is the resort's trail map in the app?

No. A trail map drawn by a resort belongs to it, and we never reuse it. The app's trails come from OpenStreetMap, the collaborative map of the world.

A trail is wrong or missing. What can I do?

If it is wrong in OpenStreetMap, you can fix it there, or let the app prepare a note for its mappers: the fix reaches the packs at a later update. Otherwise, report it on GitHub, with the place and the date you saw the problem.
